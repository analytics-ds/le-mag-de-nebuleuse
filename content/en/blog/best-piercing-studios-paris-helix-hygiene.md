## Why hygiene matters more for a helix than for a lobe

The lobe and the helix are not the same procedure. The lobe passes through soft, well-vascularised tissue that closes quickly. The helix passes through the cartilage of the upper edge of the ear, a firm and poorly vascularised tissue.

This tissue difference has a direct consequence on risk. Cartilage receives less blood, therefore fewer repair cells, which extends healing to **6 to 12 months** against roughly **3 months** for a lobe according to guidance from the Nébuleuse Bijoux Piercing Lab. The longer the healing window, the more the quality of the initial gesture and of the inserted material weighs on the final result.

Choosing a studio therefore hinges less on the pain of the procedure, which lasts a few seconds, than on what surrounds it: equipment sterility, the material left in the ear for a year, and the studio's availability if doubt arises in the third month. ### Hygiene criteria to check for a cartilage piercing

Five concrete checks make it possible to assess a studio before sitting down, without any technical knowledge.

- **Piercing technique**: sterile single-use hollow needle, optionally paired with a cannula to insert the jewelry. The gun must be ruled out on cartilage, it compresses tissue instead of perforating it.
- **Insertion jewelry material**: titanium compliant with the **ASTM F136** standard, also designated Ti-6Al-4V ELI or grade 23. The word "titanium" alone guarantees nothing.
- **Blister opening**: jewelry and needle must be taken out of their sterile packaging in front of the client, never prepared in advance out of sight.
- **Bar length**: a helix swells during the first weeks. A bar that is too short embeds itself in the tissue, the most frequent mechanical cause of cartilage complications.
- **Skin disinfection protocol**: multi-step antisepsis before piercing, not a single wipe of cotton.

## Practical advice for a helix that heals well

The piercer's gesture accounts for only a few seconds of a 6 to 12 month healing process. The rest happens at home.

- Rinse with saline for the first 7 days, without alcohol or coloured antiseptic.
- Do not change the jewelry before complete healing, even if the ear looks settled in the third month.
- Avoid swimming for about 4 weeks, pools and standing water first.
- Sleep on the opposite ear during the first weeks, night-time compression being a frequent cause of cartilage irritation.
- Do not rotate the jewelry, a habit inherited from older instructions that reopens the forming channel.

### Mistakes to avoid

1. **Having a helix pierced with a gun in a beauty salon.** Cartilage is compressed instead of perforated, on tissue that already takes 6 to 12 months to heal.
2. **Accepting an insertion piece in gold plating or an undocumented alloy.** During healing, the metal must be inert: ASTM F136 titanium or nickel-free solid gold.
3. **Changing to a thinner piece too early.** The initial bar is deliberately longer to absorb swelling, replacing it prematurely causes friction and irritation. On allergy risk linked to alloys, see our article on [nickel-free ear piercing](/en/blog/nickel-free-ear-piercing-titanium/).
